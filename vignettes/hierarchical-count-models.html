Hierarchical count models

Counts are often grouped: crash counts by road segment and year, segments within counties, animal counts by site and survey. Observations in the same group share unmeasured conditions, so they are correlated, and the groups themselves are of interest. countreg_hier() fits hierarchical (mixed-effects, multilevel) count models with normally distributed random effects at any number of grouping levels, nested or crossed, for any count family of the package.

library(flexCountReg)
data("stream_salamanders")
s <- stream_salamanders
s$sample <- factor(s$sample)

The salamander data have 644 counts from 23 stream sites, each surveyed four times (sample), for seven species and life stages (spp), at sites affected or not by mountaintop mining (mined).

1. A random intercept

Random-effect terms are written as in lme4: (1 | site) gives each site its own intercept, \(b_j \sim N(0, \sigma^2)\):

h <- countreg_hier(count ~ spp + mined + (1 | site), data = s,
                   family = "NB2")
summary(h)

The model is

\[ \log \mu_{ij} = x_{ij}'\beta + b_j, \qquad y_{ij} \mid b_j \sim \text{NB2}(\mu_{ij}, \alpha), \qquad b_j \sim N(0, \sigma^2), \]

and the likelihood integrates each site’s \(b_j\) out. With one grouping factor the integral is computed by adaptive Gauss-Hermite quadrature (9 points by default), which is accurate even for small groups.

The standard deviation 0.54 means that sites with the same covariates differ in their expected counts by a factor of about \(\exp(2 \times 0.54) \approx 3\) between the 16th and 84th percentiles. re_lrtest() tests whether the variance is zero. The null value is on the boundary of the parameter space, so the p-value is half that of the usual \(\chi^2_1\) test:

re_lrtest(h)
#>         term logLik_without logLik_with statistic df      p.value
#> 1 (1 | site)       -837.653   -826.1554  22.99518  1 8.120391e-07

2. Effects of the groups

ranef() returns the conditional modes of the site effects, the most likely value of each site’s effect given its counts. They are shrunk towards zero, more so for sites with little information. VarCorr() returns the estimated variance components and fixef() the fixed coefficients:

head(ranef(h)$site)
#>     (Intercept)
#> R-1  0.20312721
#> R-2  0.08408975
#> R-3 -0.05997694
#> R-4  0.47914042
#> R-5  0.09240978
#> R-6  0.38168445
VarCorr(h)
#>  Groups Name        Variance Std.Dev.
#>  site   (Intercept) 0.2968   0.5447

3. Crossed and nested effects

Several terms can be combined. Here the site effects are crossed with survey occasions shared by all sites:

h2 <- countreg_hier(count ~ spp + mined + (1 | site) + (1 | sample),
                    data = s, family = "POISSON")
VarCorr(h2)
#>  Groups Name        Variance Std.Dev.
#>  site   (Intercept) 0.33500  0.5788  
#>  sample (Intercept) 0.01012  0.1006

Nested effects, such as surveys within sites, are written (1 | site/sample), which is short for (1 | site) + (1 | site:sample). With more than one term the model uses the Laplace approximation with sparse matrices, as lme4::glmer() and glmmTMB do. It handles thousands of groups at several levels.

4. Random slopes

(1 + cover | site) gives each site its own intercept and its own effect of cover, with a correlation between them. (cover || site) makes them uncorrelated:

h3 <- countreg_hier(count ~ cover + mined + (cover || site), data = s,
                    family = "NB2")
#> Singular fit: a random-effect variance is estimated at or near zero (see summary()).
VarCorr(h3)
#>  Groups Name        Variance  Std.Dev. 
#>  site   (Intercept) 2.626e-01 5.125e-01
#>  site   cover       2.688e-16 1.640e-08

The slope’s standard deviation is estimated at zero, so cover has the same effect at every site. A variance on the boundary is a singular fit: summary() says so, and the Wald standard errors of that term’s variance components are not reported, since they are not meaningful there. Remove the term, or test it with re_lrtest().

Group-level predictors (such as mined, constant within sites) are ordinary columns of the formula, and cross-level interactions such as mined:cover are ordinary interactions.

5. Predictions

predict() conditions on the estimated effects of the groups by default. re.form chooses which levels to condition on (NA for none), and groups not seen in estimation get no effect:

nd <- s[c(1, 30), ]
predict(h2, newdata = nd)                         # site and sample
#> [1] 0.2103916 3.1660526
predict(h2, newdata = nd, re.form = ~ (1 | site)) # site only
#> [1] 0.2127103 3.4092077
predict(h2, newdata = nd, re.form = NA)           # population level
#> [1] 0.1958288 1.8879303

A population-level prediction with the effects at zero is the expected count of a median site. The mean over sites is larger: for the log link it is \(\exp(\eta + \sigma^2/2)\). marginal = TRUE integrates the effects that are not conditioned on out of the mean:

predict(h2, newdata = nd, re.form = NA, marginal = TRUE)
#> [1] 0.2327066 2.2434594

simulate() draws new counts, either for the same groups (use_re = "conditional") or for new groups with new effects (use_re = "new").

6. Families and engines

Every count family can be used, including user-defined families (new_count_family()), and the distribution parameters can depend on covariates (dis_param_formula_1, dis_param_formula_2). For the Poisson, NB1 and NB2 families the derivatives of the log-density are analytic; for the others they are computed numerically.

engine chooses the integration method:

engine when method
"aghq" one grouping term adaptive Gauss-Hermite quadrature, nAGQ points per effect
"laplace" any terms, nested or crossed Laplace approximation
"auto" (default) "aghq" for one term with at most two effects, otherwise "laplace"

With nAGQ = 1 the quadrature is the Laplace approximation. The estimates equal those of lme4::glmer() (Poisson) and glmmTMB (Poisson, NB1 and NB2) with the same approximation.

7. Related models

  • countreg_rp() fits random parameters at one level, with free means and non-normal distributions (lognormal, triangular, gamma, …) and heterogeneity in their means and variances, by simulation.
  • renb(), repois() and poislind_re() fit random effects with conjugate distributions in closed form.
  • countreg_fe() fits fixed-effects models, which make no assumption about how the group effects relate to the covariates.

8. Not yet available

Non-normal random effects and nested simulation, posterior predictions from a separate history of counts, profile-likelihood intervals for the variance components, smooth terms and the correlated-random-effects option (cre) are not yet available in countreg_hier().
