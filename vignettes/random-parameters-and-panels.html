Random parameters and panel models

Road segments, intersections or individuals observed repeatedly are not independent, and the effect of a variable often differs from one unit to another. flexCountReg handles both with random-parameter models (countreg.rp(), also available as countreg_rp()) and random-effects models (renb(), poisLind.re(), repois() and negmultinom()).

library(flexCountReg)

Why the panel structure matters: repeated seizure counts

MASS::epil records epileptic seizure counts for 59 patients in a clinical trial of progabide (Thall and Vail, 1990). Each patient was observed in four consecutive two-week periods, so there are four rows per patient. The independent unit is the patient, not the row.

epil <- MASS::epil
c(rows = nrow(epil), patients = length(unique(epil$subject)))
#>     rows patients 
#>      236       59
table(table(epil$subject))      # rows per patient
#> 
#>  4 
#> 59
# treatment does not change within a patient
all(tapply(epil$trt, epil$subject, function(v) length(unique(v))) == 1)
#> [1] TRUE

The response y is the count in a two-week follow-up period. The count in the eight-week baseline period before treatment (base) is a predictor, not a response: MASS provides it as lbase, the centred logarithm of the baseline count divided by 4 (a two-week equivalent), and the age as lage, the centred log age. V4 marks the fourth visit. (The data in MASS 7.3-65 and later include a documented correction to one count.)

A pooled model treats the 236 rows as independent; a random-effects model (renb(), the negative binomial model of Hausman, Hall and Griliches, 1984) gives each patient a shared effect:

f_epil <- y ~ trt + lbase + lage + V4
pooled <- countreg(f_epil, data = epil, family = "NB2", method = "BFGS")
pooled_robust <- countreg(f_epil, data = epil, family = "NB2",
                          method = "BFGS", stderr = "robust")
panel <- renb(f_epil, group_var = "subject", data = epil, method = "BFGS")
se_trt <- function(m) sqrt(diag(vcov(m)))[["trtprogabide"]]
data.frame(
  model = c("pooled NB2", "pooled NB2, robust SE", "RENB (panel)"),
  treatment_effect = c(coef(pooled)[["trtprogabide"]],
                       coef(pooled_robust)[["trtprogabide"]],
                       coef(panel)[["trtprogabide"]]),
  std_error = c(se_trt(pooled), se_trt(pooled_robust), se_trt(panel)))
#>                   model treatment_effect std_error
#> 1            pooled NB2       -0.2342385 0.1008920
#> 2 pooled NB2, robust SE       -0.2342385 0.1059424
#> 3          RENB (panel)       -0.2324608 0.1431267

The pooled model’s standard error for the treatment effect is 0.101, against 0.143 in the panel model. Rows from the same patient share that patient’s seizure tendency, so the pooled model counts four rows as four independent pieces of evidence and overstates its precision. Robust standard errors computed row by row (0.106) do not fix this: they do not group the rows by patient.

do.call(rbind, lapply(list(pooled = pooled, RENB = panel), function(m)
  glance(m)[, c("logLik", "AIC", "BIC", "nobs", "n_groups")]))
#> # A tibble: 2 × 5
#>   logLik   AIC   BIC  nobs n_groups
#> *  <dbl> <dbl> <dbl> <int>    <int>
#> 1  -651. 1314. 1335.   236       NA
#> 2  -632. 1277. 1292.   236       59

For panel models, BIC() uses the number of panels (n_groups, 59 patients) as the sample size, because the panels are the independent units. The same unit applies to every form of inference: the likelihood has one contribution per panel, robust standard errors are clustered by panel, bootstrap standard errors resample whole panels, and validation splits should keep each panel in one fold.

Random-parameter models

In a random-parameter model, a coefficient varies across units: \(\beta_i = \mu + \sigma \upsilon_i\). With group_var, all observations of a segment share one draw of the random parameter, which accounts for the panel structure. The model is estimated by maximum simulated likelihood with Halton draws; real analyses should use more draws (ndraws) than this example, and check that the estimates are stable as the number grows.

rp <- countreg_rp(Total_crashes ~ lnaadt, rpar_formula = ~ -1 + speed50,
                  data = washington_roads, family = "NB2",
                  rpardists = c(speed50 = "n"), offset = "lnlength",
                  group_var = "ID", ndraws = 100, method = "BFGS")
summary(rp)

speed50:Mean and speed50:St.Dev are the mean and standard deviation of the speed-limit effect across segments. Distributions other than the normal are available through rpardists. The two estimated parameters, labelled Mean and St.Dev, do not always equal the actual mean and standard deviation of the coefficient:

rpardists Coefficient Actual mean Actual SD
"n" normal \(\beta = \mu + \sigma z\) \(\mu\) \(\sigma\)
"ln" lognormal \(\beta = \exp(\mu + \sigma z)\) \(\exp(\mu + \sigma^2/2)\) \(\exp(\mu + \sigma^2/2)\sqrt{\exp(\sigma^2) - 1}\)
"g" gamma gamma with mean \(\mu\) and SD \(\sigma\) \(\mu\) \(\sigma\)
"t" triangular \(\mu + \sigma T\), \(T\) triangular on \([-1, 1]\) \(\mu\) \(\sigma/\sqrt{6}\)
"u" uniform \(\mu + \sigma U\), \(U\) uniform on \([-1, 1]\) \(\mu\) \(\sigma/\sqrt{3}\)

For the lognormal, \(\mu\) and \(\sigma\) are the mean and SD of \(\log\beta\), and the coefficient is positive; for a negative effect, enter the negative of the variable. For the triangular and uniform, St.Dev is a half-width: the coefficient lies in \([\mu - \sigma, \mu + \sigma]\).

Heterogeneity in means

The mean of a random parameter can depend on covariates, following Wood and Gayah (2025, Eq. 2): \(\beta_i = \mu + z_i'\delta + \sigma\upsilon_i\). Each random parameter gets its own coefficient for each heterogeneity variable:

rp_het <- countreg_rp(Total_crashes ~ lnaadt, rpar_formula = ~ -1 + speed50,
                      het_mean_formula = ~ ShouldWidth04,
                      data = washington_roads, offset = "lnlength",
                      group_var = "ID", ndraws = 100, method = "BFGS")
tidy(rp_het)

Predictions

There are three prediction methods for random-parameter models (Wood and Gayah, 2025):

  • "Exact" integrates over the random-parameter distributions analytically (with their moment generating functions), so it has no simulation error.
  • "Simulated" averages over Halton draws.
  • "Individual" (Empirical Bayes) uses each segment’s observed crashes to estimate its own coefficients. It needs the outcome, so it is for data with observed outcomes.
nd <- washington_roads[washington_roads$ID %in% unique(washington_roads$ID)[1:2], ]
cbind(observed = nd$Total_crashes,
      exact = predict(rp, newdata = nd, method = "Exact"),
      simulated = predict(rp, newdata = nd, method = "Simulated"),
      individual = predict(rp, newdata = nd, method = "Individual"))
#>      observed     exact simulated individual
#> [1,]        0 0.8546141 0.8422646  0.5316823
#> [2,]        2 0.7552404 0.7443269  1.0291883
#> [3,]        0 0.8495740 0.8372973  0.5285467
#> [4,]        0 0.7507863 0.7399372  1.0231186
#> [5,]        1 0.8957947 0.8828501  0.5573020
#> [6,]        3 0.7916325 0.7801931  1.0787808

The segment-specific coefficients from the Empirical Bayes method are returned as an attribute:

eb <- predict(rp, newdata = washington_roads, method = "Individual")
summary(attr(eb, "eb_coefficients")[, "speed50"])
#>    Min. 1st Qu.  Median    Mean 3rd Qu.    Max. 
#> -1.3126 -0.7051 -0.7051 -0.7080 -0.7051  0.1780

A caution about the expected count: with a random parameter the expected value of \(\exp(x\beta)\) must exist. It is infinite when \(\beta\) is lognormal and \(x > 0\), and when \(\beta\) is gamma and \(x\sigma^2/\mu \ge 1\). In those cases "Exact" and "Simulated" predictions return Inf with a warning (a simulated average would otherwise depend on the number of draws without converging). This is a property of the model, not a numerical failure: changing the distribution only helps if the new distribution’s expectation exists for the covariate values used. Normal, triangular and uniform parameters always have a finite expectation.

Random-effects models

Random-effects models add a panel-level effect to every observation of a unit. The negative binomial random-effects model of Hausman, Hall and Griliches (renb()), the Poisson-Lindley random-effects model (poisLind.re()) and the negative multinomial models (negmultinom()) are available:

re_models <- list(
  RENB = renb(Total_crashes ~ lnaadt + speed50, group_var = "ID",
              data = washington_roads, offset = "lnlength", method = "BFGS"),
  PoisLindRE = poisLind.re(Total_crashes ~ lnaadt + speed50, group_var = "ID",
                           data = washington_roads, offset = "lnlength",
                           method = "BFGS"),
  NM2 = negmultinom(Total_crashes ~ lnaadt + speed50, group_var = "ID",
                    data = washington_roads, offset = "lnlength")
)
#> Warning: The Hessian is singular; standard errors use a generalized inverse and
#> may be unreliable. The model may not be identified. The likelihood is flat in a
#> direction involving ln(theta); their standard errors are NA.
do.call(rbind, lapply(names(re_models), function(nm) {
  cbind(model = nm,
        glance(re_models[[nm]])[, c("logLik", "AIC", "BIC", "nobs",
                                    "n_groups")])
}))
#>        model    logLik      AIC      BIC nobs n_groups
#> 1       RENB -1070.458 2150.916 2172.059 1501      507
#> 2 PoisLindRE -1070.830 2149.660 2166.574 1501      507
#> 3        NM2 -1070.454 2148.908 2165.822 1501      507

For panel models, BIC() uses the number of panels (n_groups) as the sample size, because the panels are the independent units.

The warning above comes from the Poisson-Lindley random-effects model: its random-effect parameter is driven toward the edge of its parameter space, where the likelihood is flat, so its standard error is not identified and is reported as NA:

tidy(re_models$PoisLindRE)
#> # A tibble: 4 × 6
#>   term        component    estimate std.error statistic   p.value
#>   <chr>       <chr>           <dbl>     <dbl>     <dbl>     <dbl>
#> 1 (Intercept) mean           -8.77     0.502     -17.5   2.89e-68
#> 2 lnaadt      mean            1.11     0.0590     18.8   3.22e-79
#> 3 speed50     mean           -0.561    0.132      -4.24  2.21e- 5
#> 4 ln(theta)   distribution   -7.13    NA          NA    NA

Its fit statistics are close to those of the other two models, but with one parameter not identified its reported uncertainty is incomplete. When models fit about equally well, prefer one whose parameters are all identified, such as RENB or the negative multinomial model here.

Standard errors

The likelihood of a panel model has one contribution per panel, so robust (sandwich) standard errors are clustered by panel. Bootstrap standard errors resample whole panels:

re_robust <- poisLind.re(Total_crashes ~ lnaadt + speed50, group_var = "ID",
                         data = washington_roads, offset = "lnlength",
                         method = "BFGS", stderr = "robust")
#> Warning: The Hessian is singular; standard errors use a generalized inverse and
#> may be unreliable. The model may not be identified. The likelihood is flat in a
#> direction involving ln(theta); their standard errors are NA.
tidy(re_robust)
#> # A tibble: 4 × 6
#>   term        component    estimate std.error statistic p.value
#>   <chr>       <chr>           <dbl>     <dbl>     <dbl>   <dbl>
#> 1 (Intercept) mean           -8.77         NA        NA      NA
#> 2 lnaadt      mean            1.11         NA        NA      NA
#> 3 speed50     mean           -0.561        NA        NA      NA
#> 4 ln(theta)   distribution   -7.13         NA        NA      NA

Cross-sectional models can also be fitted with stderr = "robust" or "boot"; for repeated observations of the same units, prefer a panel model or clustered resampling.

Cross-validation

When tuning or comparing these models by cross-validation (for example with the tidymodels count_reg() specification), resample whole panels, for example with rsample::group_vfold_cv(data, group = ID). Splitting the observations of one segment between the training and test sets leaks information and makes the models look better than they are.
