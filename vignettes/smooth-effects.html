Smooth effects: generalized additive count models

A linear term such as lnaadt assumes that every unit increase in log traffic changes the log crash rate by the same amount. Often the response bends: flat at low volumes, steep in the middle, leveling off. A smooth term lets the data choose the shape, while a penalty keeps it from following noise.

flexCountReg accepts the smooth terms of the mgcv package, s(), te(), ti() and t2(), in the formulas of countreg() and countreg_rp(): the mean, the distribution parameters, the zero model and, for random parameters, the heterogeneity in their means and variances. The model is a distributional generalized additive model: any parameter of any count family can depend smoothly on covariates.

library(flexCountReg)
data("washington_roads")

1. A smooth traffic-volume effect

m <- countreg(Total_crashes ~ s(lnaadt) + speed50 + offset(lnlength),

The smooth is summarized by its effective degrees of freedom (EDF): 1 for a straight line, up to the basis dimension k - 1 for an unpenalized curve. Its coefficients are not shown one by one: they only describe the curve together. The test is an approximate test that the whole smooth is zero.

plot_smooths() shows each smooth on the scale of its linear predictor, with a pointwise band (shaded) and a simultaneous band (dashed) that covers the whole curve with 95% probability:

plot_smooths(m)

2. How smooth?

The amount of smoothing is chosen automatically. For each smooth a smoothing parameter \(\lambda\) multiplies its wiggliness penalty \(b'Sb\), and the model maximizes

\[ \ell(\theta) - \tfrac{1}{2} \sum_j \lambda_j b_j' S_j b_j . \]

The \(\lambda_j\) are selected by generalized Fellner-Schall updates (Wood and Fasiolo 2017), which approximately maximize the restricted marginal likelihood (REML). They are stored in the fit, and sp fixes them:

m$model$smooth_lambda
#> [1] 0.007341266
stiff <- countreg(Total_crashes ~ s(lnaadt) + speed50 + offset(lnlength),
                  data = washington_roads, family = "NB2", sp = 100)
stiff$model$smooth_table[, c("term", "edf", "lambda")]
#>        term      edf lambda
#> 1 s(lnaadt) 1.090613    100

A large \(\lambda\) shrinks the smooth to its unpenalized part (here a straight line). The log-likelihood’s degrees of freedom are the total EDF, so AIC() compares smooth and parametric models fairly:

lin <- countreg(Total_crashes ~ lnaadt + speed50 + offset(lnlength),
                data = washington_roads, family = "NB2", method = "BFGS")
c(linear = AIC(lin), smooth = AIC(m))
#>   linear   smooth 
#> 2189.118 2133.014

3. Smooth distribution parameters and surfaces

Any distribution parameter can be smooth. Here the NB2 dispersion may vary smoothly with segment length, and traffic and length interact through a tensor-product surface te():

m2 <- countreg(Total_crashes ~ te(lnaadt, lnlength) + speed50,
               data = washington_roads, family = "NB2",
               dis_param_formula_1 = ~ s(lnlength, k = 5))
m2$model$smooth_table[, c("parameter", "term", "edf", "p.value")]
#>   parameter                term      edf      p.value
#> 1      mean te(lnaadt,lnlength) 7.929393 1.617836e-89
#> 2 ln(alpha)         s(lnlength) 1.000000 3.758677e-01
op <- par(mfrow = c(1, 2))
plot_smooths(m2)

par(op)

The dispersion smooth has shrunk to a straight line (EDF 1): there is no evidence that the overdispersion changes nonlinearly with length.

4. Smooths with random parameters

countreg_rp() accepts smooth terms in its fixed part and in the heterogeneity formulas. Three ideas look alike but are different:

  • a random parameter (rpar_formula) is a coefficient that varies across sites with an unknown distribution, integrated by simulation;
  • a smooth is a nonlinear function of an observed variable;
  • a smooth heterogeneity in means (het_mean_formula = ~ s(z)) lets a random parameter’s mean change smoothly with an observed site attribute.

They can be combined:

rp <- countreg_rp(Total_crashes ~ s(lnaadt, k = 8) + offset(lnlength),
                  rpar_formula = ~ -1 + speed50, data = washington_roads,
                  family = "NB2", group_var = "ID", ndraws = 50)
summary(rp)

A smooth can absorb heterogeneity that a random parameter’s standard deviation would otherwise carry, so compare the random parameters’ standard deviations with and without the smooth. Smooth terms are not allowed in rpar_formula: a random coefficient on each basis column is not a penalized smooth.

5. Smooths in random-effects panel models

The random-effects panel models renb(), repois(), poislind_re() and negmultinom() accept smooth terms in the same way, with the panels as the independent units:

re <- repois(Total_crashes ~ s(lnaadt, k = 8) + speed50 + offset(lnlength),
             group_var = "ID", data = washington_roads)
re$model$smooth_table[, c("term", "edf", "lambda", "p.value")]
#>        term      edf lambda      p.value
#> 1 s(lnaadt) 4.986079 0.0718 4.214872e-71

Fixed-effects models (countreg_fe()) accept smooths of variables that change within units, such as s(lnaadt). A smooth of a variable that is constant within units is absorbed by the fixed effects, and is refused.

Finite mixtures and latent-class models (countreg_mix(), countreg_lc()) give each component its own smooth, and the class membership can also be smooth (mixing_formula = ~ s(x), or membership_formula in countreg_lc()):

mx <- countreg_mix(Total_crashes ~ s(lnaadt, k = 8) + offset(lnlength),
                   data = washington_roads, family = "POISSON",
                   components = 2, n_starts = 3, seed = 1)
mx$model$smooth_table[, c("parameter", "term", "edf", "lambda")]
#>         parameter      term      edf lambda
#> 1 Component1:mean s(lnaadt) 5.257868  0.063
#> 2 Component2:mean s(lnaadt) 1.000000 356000

6. Inference and agreement with mgcv

Standard errors are Bayesian by default: the covariance of the estimates is \((H + S_\lambda)^{-1}\), with \(H\) the negative Hessian of the log-likelihood and \(S_\lambda\) the total penalty. Robust (stderr = "robust") and cluster-robust (cluster = ~ site) versions use the same bread.

With the smoothing parameters fixed at those of mgcv::gam(), the estimates, log-likelihood, EDF and standard errors equal mgcv’s for the Poisson and negative binomial models (with the dispersion fixed). The selected smoothing parameters are close to mgcv’s REML values (within about 5% in these data); the Fellner-Schall update ignores how the Hessian changes with \(\lambda\), which REML includes.

The tests of smooth terms are approximate (they follow Wood 2013 but ignore the uncertainty in \(\lambda\)), and the log-likelihood’s degrees of freedom are the EDF without the smoothing-parameter correction that mgcv::logLik.gam() adds.
