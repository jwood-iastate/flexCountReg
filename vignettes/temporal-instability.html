Time-varying effects: temporal instability

With several years of data, the effect of a variable may change over time: vehicles, enforcement and driver behavior change while the roads stay the same. This is temporal instability. tv(x, time = year) in a model formula gives x one effect per year, and temporal_effects() and temporal_tests() report and test those effects.

library(flexCountReg)
data("washington_roads")
w <- washington_roads
table(w$Year)
#> 
#> 2016 2017 2018 
#>  501  500  500

1. One effect per year

m <- countreg(Total_crashes ~ tv(lnaadt, time = Year) + speed50 +
                offset(lnlength), data = w, family = "NB2",
              method = "BFGS")
coef(m)
#>          (Intercept)      lnaadt:Year2016 lnaadt:Year_step2017 
#>          -8.89992454           1.13027036          -0.00657049 
#> lnaadt:Year_step2018              speed50            ln(alpha) 
#>          -0.00318521          -0.56770930          -0.92122379

The default step basis has two kinds of coefficients:

  • lnaadt:Year2016 is the effect in the first year;
  • lnaadt:Year_step2017 and lnaadt:Year_step2018 are the changes from the year before.

A change near zero means the effect is the same in two adjacent years. temporal_effects() gives the effect in each year, with its standard error and confidence interval:

temporal_effects(m)
#>   part   term time basis period estimate  std.error conf.low conf.high
#> 1 mean lnaadt Year  step   2016 1.130270 0.05200214 1.028348  1.232193
#> 2 mean lnaadt Year  step   2017 1.123700 0.05201507 1.021752  1.225648
#> 3 mean lnaadt Year  step   2018 1.120515 0.05185510 1.018881  1.222149

temporal_tests() tests whether the effects are all equal, and whether each adjacent pair of years differs:

temporal_tests(m)
#>   part   term         test    estimate  std.error  statistic df   p.value
#> 1 mean lnaadt    all equal          NA         NA  0.5951265  2 0.7426256
#> 2 mean lnaadt 2017 vs 2016 -0.00657049 0.01297047 -0.5065731  1 0.6124544
#> 3 mean lnaadt 2018 vs 2017 -0.00318521 0.01308643 -0.2433979  1 0.8076972

Here the effect of traffic is stable over the three years.

2. Choosing the basis

The basis decides what each coefficient means. The fit and the year effects are the same for every basis.

basis effect in year \(t\) a zero coefficient means
"step" (default) \(\beta_1 + \sum_{k=2}^t \Delta_k\) no change between years \(k-1\) and \(k\)
"level" \(\beta_t\) no effect in year \(t\)
"hinge" \(\sum_k \max(t-k+1, 0)\,\delta_k\) the earlier trend continues at year \(k\)
"smooth" \(\beta(t)\), a penalized spline (the smoothness is estimated) 
fit_basis <- function(b) {
  countreg(stats::as.formula(paste0(
    "Total_crashes ~ tv(lnaadt, time = Year, basis = '", b,
    "') + speed50 + offset(lnlength)")),
    data = w, family = "NB2", method = "BFGS")
}
mh <- fit_basis("hinge")
c(step = logLik(m), hinge = logLik(mh))
#>      step     hinge 
#> -1090.263 -1090.263
cbind(step = temporal_effects(m)$estimate,
      hinge = temporal_effects(mh)$estimate)
#>          step    hinge
#> [1,] 1.130270 1.130270
#> [2,] 1.123700 1.123699
#> [3,] 1.120515 1.120514

A caution about the hinge basis

The hinge basis is the “spline indicator” model of Shabab et al. (2026). Its columns are cumulative: the indicator for year \(k\) is \(\max(t - k + 1, 0)\). As a result, \(\delta_2 = \beta_2 - 2\beta_1\). It is not \(\beta_2 - \beta_1\).

Dropping a hinge coefficient that is not significant therefore does not make two years equal. It continues the trend of the earlier years. With the paper’s own AADT estimates:

year separate effects dropping \(\delta_2\) imposes
1 0.122 0.122
2 0.215 \(2 \times 0.122 = 0.244\)

Equal effects in years 1 and 2 would be 0.122 in both. The basis also numbers the years from 1, so the restriction depends on which year comes first. To find years with equal effects, use the step basis, where a zero coefficient means exactly that, or temporal_tests(), which tests the year effects themselves.

A smooth path over time

With many years, basis = "smooth" fits the effect as a penalized spline in time, the varying-coefficient smooth s(Year, by = lnaadt) of mgcv. The data then choose between a constant effect, a linear trend and a flexible path:

msm <- fit_basis("smooth")
temporal_effects(msm)
#>   part   term time  basis period estimate  std.error conf.low conf.high
#> 1 mean lnaadt Year smooth   2016 1.129769 0.05189659 1.028053  1.231484
#> 2 mean lnaadt Year smooth   2017 1.124881 0.05141355 1.024112  1.225650
#> 3 mean lnaadt Year smooth   2018 1.119993 0.05174021 1.018584  1.221402

3. Time-varying intercepts and distribution parameters

tv(1, time = Year) lets the intercept vary by year. With an intercept in the model, the intercept is the first year’s level and the tv() columns are the changes after it. tv() also works in the formulas of the distribution parameters, here a separate NB2 overdispersion for each year:

mi <- countreg(Total_crashes ~ tv(1, time = Year) + lnaadt +
                 offset(lnlength), data = w, family = "NB2",
               dis_param_formula_1 = ~ tv(1, time = Year, basis = "level"),
               method = "BFGS")
temporal_effects(mi)
#>        part        term time basis period   estimate std.error   conf.low
#> 1      mean (Intercept) Year  step   2016 -9.3636110 0.4574756 -10.260247
#> 2      mean (Intercept) Year  step   2017 -9.4307156 0.4588819 -10.330108
#> 3      mean (Intercept) Year  step   2018 -9.4248184 0.4570932 -10.320705
#> 4 ln(alpha) (Intercept) Year level   2016 -0.8703054 0.3746837  -1.604672
#> 5 ln(alpha) (Intercept) Year level   2017 -1.1701419 0.4819031  -2.114655
#> 6 ln(alpha) (Intercept) Year level   2018 -0.4144498 0.3105474  -1.023112
#>    conf.high

4. Prediction for new years

predict() uses the years of the estimation data. A year that was not estimated is an error by default, because no effect was estimated for it. tv(x, time = Year, new_time = "nearest") uses the nearest year’s effect instead, for example the last year’s effect when forecasting:

mn <- countreg(Total_crashes ~ tv(lnaadt, time = Year, new_time = "nearest") +
                 speed50 + offset(lnlength), data = w, family = "NB2",
               method = "BFGS")
nd <- w[1:3, ]
nd$Year <- 2019
predict(mn, newdata = nd)
#> [1] 0.7657030 0.6766678 1.1218439

5. Other models

tv() works in the formulas of every estimator: countreg(), countreg_rp(), countreg_hier(), countreg_fe(), renb(), repois(), poislind_re() and countreg_mix(). Here the effect of traffic varies by year in a random-parameters model:

r <- countreg_rp(Total_crashes ~ tv(lnaadt, time = Year) + offset(lnlength),
                 rpar_formula = ~ -1 + speed50, data = w, family = "NB2",
                 ndraws = 100, method = "BFGS", group_var = "ID")
temporal_effects(r)
#>   part   term time basis period estimate  std.error conf.low conf.high
#> 1 mean lnaadt Year  step   2016 1.130707 0.05300066 1.026827  1.234586
#> 2 mean lnaadt Year  step   2017 1.123370 0.05302431 1.019444  1.227296
#> 3 mean lnaadt Year  step   2018 1.121112 0.05277747 1.017670  1.224554

tv() can also appear in rpar_formula. temporal_effects() then reports the year effects of the random parameters’ means, and each basis column has its own standard deviation.

Reference

Shabab, K. R., Bhowmik, T., Zaki, M. H. and Eluru, N. (2026). A joint count and generalized ordered fractional split approach for addressing temporal instability in road safety analysis. Analytic Methods in Accident Research, 51, 100440.
