Count models with tidymodels

tidymodels is a family of packages for building, resampling, tuning and comparing models with one interface. flexCountReg plugs into it through parsnip: count_reg() is a model specification, and every estimator of the package is one of its engines. A negative binomial regression, a latent-class model and a random-effects model can then share the same preprocessing, the same resamples and the same metrics.

This article covers fitting and predicting, recipes and workflows, offsets and other special columns, comparing and tuning models, weights, panel models, several outcomes, and getting back to the functions of flexCountReg.

library(parsnip)
library(recipes)
library(workflows)
library(workflowsets)
library(rsample)
library(tune)
library(yardstick)
library(flexCountReg)

data("washington_roads")
roads <- tibble::as_tibble(washington_roads)
roads$ID <- as.character(roads$ID)

library(tidymodels) loads all of these except flexCountReg at once. The data have three years of crashes on each road segment, identified by ID.

A first model

Choose the distribution with family, the estimator with set_engine(), and fit. Arguments of the estimator go into set_engine().

nb_spec <- count_reg(family = "NB2") |>
  set_engine("flexCountReg", method = "BFGS")

nb_fit <- fit(nb_spec, Total_crashes ~ lnaadt + lnlength + speed50,
              data = roads)
tidy(nb_fit)

Two engine arguments belong to the tidymodels interface:

  • predict_args is a list of arguments for the model’s predict() method, used for numeric predictions, for example predict_args = list(method = "Exact") for random parameters.
  • fit_engine is the estimators’ own engine argument (the numerical method), because set_engine() already has an argument of that name.

Recipes and workflows

A workflow bundles preprocessing with the model. Here a recipe centers and scales the numeric predictors and makes indicators of a factor; the statistics are estimated on the training data only.

roads$year <- factor(roads$Year)
set.seed(2026)
split <- group_initial_split(roads, group = ID, prop = 0.75)
train <- training(split)
test <- testing(split)

rec <- recipe(Total_crashes ~ lnaadt + lnlength + speed50 + year,
              data = train) |>
  step_normalize(lnaadt, lnlength) |>
  step_dummy(year)

nb_wf <- workflow() |>
  add_recipe(rec) |>
  add_model(nb_spec)
nb_wf_fit <- fit(nb_wf, train)
tidy(nb_wf_fit)
#> # A tibble: 7 × 6
#>   term        component    estimate std.error statistic  p.value
#>   <chr>       <chr>           <dbl>     <dbl>     <dbl>    <dbl>
#> 1 (Intercept) mean           -1.12     0.104     -10.8  5.33e-27
#> 2 lnaadt      mean            1.11     0.0605     18.3  1.30e-74
#> 3 lnlength    mean            0.532    0.0536      9.94 2.82e-23
#> 4 speed50     mean           -0.556    0.125      -4.45 8.41e- 6
#> 5 year_X2017  mean           -0.182    0.124      -1.47 1.42e- 1
#> 6 year_X2018  mean           -0.155    0.122      -1.27 2.04e- 1
#> 7 ln(alpha)   distribution   -1.05     0.281      -3.75 1.79e- 4

augment(nb_wf_fit, test) |>
  rmse(truth = Total_crashes, estimate = .pred)
#> # A tibble: 1 × 3
#>   .metric .estimator .estimate
#>   <chr>   <chr>          <dbl>
#> 1 rmse    standard       0.734

The split keeps all years of a segment on one side (group_initial_split()), so the test segments are new to the model. A random split of rows would put years of one segment on both sides.

flexCountReg’s own rmse() and mae() take two vectors; given a data frame they call the yardstick metric, so tidymodels code works whichever package is loaded last. (With yardstick loaded last, use flexCountReg::rmse() or yardstick::rmse_vec() for two vectors.)

Offsets and other columns that are not predictors

With add_formula() or a recipe, all predictors enter the mean model. Count models often need columns with another purpose: an exposure, a grouping variable, or a covariate of the dispersion only. A workflow passes the model only its predictors and outcomes, so these columns must be among the predictors (a recipe’s other roles do not reach the model). There are two ways to say what they are.

Give the model formula in add_model(). The preprocessor says which columns are available and the formula says how they are used. All the formula features of the package work here: offset(), smooths s(x), tv() terms and random effects.

wf_offset <- workflow() |>
  add_variables(outcomes = Total_crashes,
                predictors = c(lnaadt, lnlength, speed50)) |>
  add_model(count_reg() |>
              set_engine("flexCountReg", method = "BFGS",
                         dis_param_formula_1 = ~ lnlength),
            formula = Total_crashes ~ lnaadt + speed50 + offset(lnlength))
tidy(fit(wf_offset, train))[, 1:4]
#> # A tibble: 5 × 4
#>   term                  component    estimate std.error
#>   <chr>                 <chr>           <dbl>     <dbl>
#> 1 (Intercept)           mean           -8.68     0.510 
#> 2 lnaadt                mean            1.10     0.0591
#> 3 speed50               mean           -0.565    0.127 
#> 4 ln(alpha):(Intercept) distribution   -1.25     0.470 
#> 5 ln(alpha):lnlength    distribution   -0.372    0.390

A recipe works too, in place of add_variables():

workflow() |>
  add_recipe(rec) |>
  add_model(nb_spec, formula = Total_crashes ~ s(lnaadt) + speed50)

Name the columns in engine arguments. Columns named in offset, group_var, panel_id, cluster, fixef, cre_group or time are left out of the mean model when no model formula is given. So are the variables of rpar_formula with the countreg_rp engine, which are random instead of fixed.

wf_offset2 <- workflow() |>
  add_formula(Total_crashes ~ lnaadt + lnlength) |>
  add_model(count_reg() |> set_engine("flexCountReg", method = "BFGS",
                                      offset = "lnlength"))
tidy(fit(wf_offset2, train))[, 1:4]
#> # A tibble: 3 × 4
#>   term        component    estimate std.error
#>   <chr>       <chr>           <dbl>     <dbl>
#> 1 (Intercept) mean           -9.24     0.507 
#> 2 lnaadt      mean            1.15     0.0590
#> 3 ln(alpha)   distribution   -0.840    0.252

Variables used only in dis_param_formula_1, zero_formula and similar formulas are not left out, because they often belong in both places; use a model formula for those. Do not normalize an offset or make indicators of a grouping column in a recipe.

Comparing models on resamples

Cross-validate with whole segments, and compare several engines in a workflow set. The panel engines receive the segment as group_var.

set.seed(2026)
folds <- group_vfold_cv(train, group = ID, v = 3)

specs <- list(
  poisson = count_reg(family = "POISSON") |>
    set_engine("flexCountReg", method = "BFGS"),
  nb2 = nb_spec,
  two_class = count_reg(family = "POISSON") |>
    set_engine("countreg_lc", classes = 2, n_starts = 3, seed = 1,
               group_var = "ID"),
  re_poisson = count_reg() |>
    set_engine("repois", group_var = "ID", method = "BFGS"))

base <- workflow_set(
  preproc = list(base = Total_crashes ~ lnaadt + lnlength + speed50 + ID),
  models = specs[c("two_class", "re_poisson")])
plain <- workflow_set(
  preproc = list(base = Total_crashes ~ lnaadt + lnlength + speed50),
  models = specs[c("poisson", "nb2")])

res <- dplyr::bind_rows(base, plain) |>
  workflow_map("fit_resamples", resamples = folds,
               metrics = metric_set(rmse, mae))
rank_results(res, rank_metric = "rmse")[, c("wflow_id", ".metric", "mean",
                                            "std_err")]
#> # A tibble: 8 × 4
#>   wflow_id        .metric  mean std_err
#>   <chr>           <chr>   <dbl>   <dbl>
#> 1 base_two_class  mae     0.498  0.0294
#> 2 base_two_class  rmse    0.843  0.0946
#> 3 base_re_poisson mae     0.498  0.0261
#> 4 base_re_poisson rmse    0.850  0.0932
#> 5 base_nb2        mae     0.497  0.0261
#> 6 base_nb2        rmse    0.851  0.0934
#> 7 base_poisson    mae     0.497  0.0253
#> 8 base_poisson    rmse    0.851  0.0919

RMSE and MAE only look at the expected count, and models with the same mean structure are hard to tell apart by them. To compare the distributions, score the predicted probabilities. fit_resamples_counts() resamples a workflow or specification like fit_resamples() and can add the mean log probability of the held-out counts (larger is better). See vignette("predictive-validation", package = "flexCountReg") for scores and calibration.

f <- Total_crashes ~ lnaadt + lnlength + speed50
scores <- lapply(specs, fit_resamples_counts, resamples = folds,
                 formula = f, log_score = TRUE)
do.call(rbind, Map(function(s, nm) {
  cbind(model = nm, s[s$.metric == "log_score", c("mean", "std_err")])
}, scores, names(scores)))
#>                 model    mean std_err
#> poisson       poisson -0.7709 0.04543
#> nb2               nb2 -0.7518 0.04256
#> two_class   two_class -0.7606 0.05090
#> re_poisson re_poisson -0.7510 0.04285

Tuning

The distribution is a tuning parameter:

tune_wf <- workflow() |>
  add_formula(Total_crashes ~ lnaadt + lnlength + speed50) |>
  add_model(count_reg(family = tune()) |>
              set_engine("flexCountReg", method = "BFGS"))

tuned <- tune_grid(tune_wf, resamples = folds,
                   grid = data.frame(family = c("POISSON", "NB1", "NB2",
                                                "PL")),
                   metrics = metric_set(rmse, mae))
show_best(tuned, metric = "rmse")
#> # A tibble: 4 × 7

Some engine arguments have tuning parameters too (see ?count_family): components and classes, zero_type, correlated and re_dist. Any other argument can be tuned with an explicit grid.

lc_wf <- workflow() |>
  add_formula(Total_crashes ~ lnaadt + lnlength) |>
  add_model(count_reg(family = "POISSON") |>
              set_engine("countreg_mix", components = tune(), n_starts = 2,
                         seed = 1))
hardhat::extract_parameter_set_dials(lc_wf)
tune_grid(lc_wf, folds, grid = data.frame(components = 2:3),
          metrics = metric_set(rmse)) |>
  collect_metrics()
#> # A tibble: 2 × 7
#>   components .metric .estimator  mean     n std_err .config        
#>        <int> <chr>   <chr>      <dbl> <int>   <dbl> <chr>          
#> 1          2 rmse    standard   0.839     3  0.0858 pre0_mod1_post0
#> 2          3 rmse    standard   0.843     3  0.0895 pre0_mod2_post0

Tuning selects for out-of-sample accuracy; the standard errors of the selected model do not account for the selection. Some families have no dispersion parameter, so a grid that includes "POISSON" fails for those rows when dis_param_formula_1 is set.

Use a parallel backend for slow models (future::plan("multisession") before tune_grid()); the workers load flexCountReg themselves.

Case weights

Case weights become the estimator’s weights, by default frequency weights. parsnip passes the engine only the values of the weights, so for importance or sampling weights say so with weight_type = "sampling" (flexCountReg and countreg_rp engines), which gives robust standard errors.

train_w <- train
train_w$w <- hardhat::frequency_weights(rep(1:2, length.out = nrow(train)))
wf_w <- workflow() |>
  add_formula(Total_crashes ~ lnaadt + lnlength) |>
  add_case_weights(w) |>
  add_model(nb_spec)
tidy(fit(wf_w, train_w))[, 1:4]
#> # A tibble: 4 × 4
#>   term        component    estimate std.error
#>   <chr>       <chr>           <dbl>     <dbl>
#> 1 (Intercept) mean           -9.27     0.416 
#> 2 lnaadt      mean            1.12     0.0493
#> 3 lnlength    mean            0.765    0.0646
#> 4 ln(alpha)   distribution   -1.08     0.245

Panel and hierarchical models

Random effects of countreg_hier() are written in the model formula:

hier_wf <- workflow() |>
  add_variables(outcomes = Total_crashes,
                predictors = c(lnaadt, lnlength, ID)) |>
  add_model(count_reg(family = "NB2") |>
              set_engine("countreg_hier",
                         predict_args = list(marginal = TRUE)),
            formula = Total_crashes ~ lnaadt + lnlength + (1 | ID))
hier_fit <- fit(hier_wf, train)
predict(hier_fit, test[1:3, ])
#> # A tibble: 3 × 1
#>   .pred
#>   <dbl>
#> 1 1.27 
#> 2 1.03 
#> 3 0.476

The test segments are new groups. With marginal = TRUE their predictions average over the distribution of the random effect; without it they are for a segment whose effect is zero. The choice matters for rmse(), so set it in predict_args. Likewise, countreg_fe() has no effect for a new group and predicts NA unless predict_args = list(newgroup = "mean_fe").

A random-parameters model:

rp_spec <- count_reg(family = "NB2") |>
  set_engine("countreg_rp", rpar_formula = ~ -1 + lnlength,
             ndraws = 100, predict_args = list(method = "Exact"))
rp_wf <- workflow() |>
  add_formula(Total_crashes ~ lnaadt + speed50 + lnlength) |>
  add_model(rp_spec)
rp_fit <- fit(rp_wf, train)
tidy(rp_fit)[, 1:4]

Several outcomes

countreg_mv() and countreg_alloc() model several counts. Declare all of them as outcomes and give the model formula. There is one prediction column per outcome.

set.seed(1)
sev <- train
sev$injury <- rbinom(nrow(sev), sev$Total_crashes, 0.35)
sev$pdo <- sev$Total_crashes - sev$injury

mv_wf <- workflow() |>
  add_variables(outcomes = c(injury, pdo),
                predictors = c(lnaadt, lnlength)) |>
  add_model(count_reg(family = "NB2") |>
              set_engine("countreg_mv", copula = "frank"),
            formula = cbind(injury, pdo) ~ lnaadt + lnlength)
mv_fit <- fit(mv_wf, sev)
predict(mv_fit, sev[1:3, ])
#> # A tibble: 3 × 2
#>   .pred_injury .pred_pdo
#>          <dbl>     <dbl>
#> 1        0.403     0.805
#> 2        0.366     0.730
#> 3        0.542     1.09

(The severities here are simulated.) Interval predictions have a pair of columns per outcome:

predict(mv_fit, sev[1:3, ], type = "pred_int", level = 0.9)
#> # A tibble: 3 × 4
#>   .pred_lower_injury .pred_upper_injury .pred_lower_pdo .pred_upper_pdo

Back to flexCountReg

extract_fit_engine() returns the flexCountReg model, for everything that tidymodels has no interface for: marginal effects, CURE plots, rootograms, robust covariances, model tests.

Keep in mind:

  • Without a model formula the outcome is called ..y in the extracted model; with one it keeps its name.
  • The extracted model sees the preprocessed columns. Marginal effects after step_normalize() are per standard deviation.
  • A workflow removes the outcome from new_data. Predictions that use it, such as Empirical Bayes predictions (predict(m, newdata, method = "Individual")) or posterior class probabilities, need the extracted model.
  • keep_data = FALSE (an engine argument of most estimators) makes saved models smaller, at the cost of the functions that need the training data.
  • Load flexCountReg before using a saved workflow: that registers the engines.
